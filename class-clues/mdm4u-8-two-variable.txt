## COEFFICIENT OF DETERMINATION
1 | The coefficient of determination is written as this | r² | r; √r; 1 − r
2 | r = 0.9. What is r²? | = 0.81 ~0.5%
3 | r² = 0.64 means about this percent of the variation in y is explained by x | = 64 % ~0
4 | r² = 0.49 and the correlation is negative. What is r? | = -0.7 ~0.5%
5 | An r² close to 0 means this | THE LINE EXPLAINS LITTLE OF THE VARIATION | THE LINE FITS PERFECTLY; THE SLOPE IS ZERO; THERE ARE NO RESIDUALS

## CORRELATION OR CAUSATION?
1 | Ice cream sales and drownings both rise on hot days. Does ice cream cause drowning? | NO | YES; ONLY ON WEEKENDS; ONLY AT BEACHES
2 | In the ice cream and drowning data, temperature is an example of this | A LURKING (HIDDEN) VARIABLE | THE DEPENDENT VARIABLE; A RESIDUAL; AN OUTLIER
3 | When both variables are caused by a third variable, the relationship is called this | COMMON-CAUSE FACTOR | CAUSE AND EFFECT; REVERSE CAUSE AND EFFECT; ACCIDENTAL
4 | A strong correlation between two unrelated things, like cheese eaten and lawyers in a town, by pure chance is called this | ACCIDENTAL RELATIONSHIP | CAUSE AND EFFECT; COMMON-CAUSE FACTOR; REVERSE CAUSE AND EFFECT
5 | People with more fire trucks at a fire have more damage, but big fires bring more trucks. Saying trucks cause damage is this mistake | REVERSE CAUSE AND EFFECT | ACCIDENTAL RELATIONSHIP; PRESUMED RELATIONSHIP; NO MISTAKE
// The Ontario MDM4U types: cause and effect, common-cause factor, reverse cause and effect, accidental, presumed.

## TYPES OF RELATIONSHIPS
1 | When a change in x directly produces a change in y, the relationship is this | CAUSE AND EFFECT / CAUSE-AND-EFFECT / CAUSAL
2 | A relationship that seems logical but has no clear causal link and isn't accidental is called this | PRESUMED RELATIONSHIP | ACCIDENTAL RELATIONSHIP; COMMON-CAUSE FACTOR; REVERSE CAUSE AND EFFECT
3 | The best way to show that x causes y is this | A CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT | A SCATTER PLOT; A HIGH r VALUE; A LARGE SURVEY
4 | A variable that is mixed up with the independent variable, so their effects can't be separated, is called this | CONFOUNDING / CONFOUNDING VARIABLE / A CONFOUNDING VARIABLE
5 | Shoe size and reading ability are correlated in children. The most likely explanation is this | AGE IS A COMMON CAUSE | BIG FEET HELP READING; READING MAKES FEET GROW; IT IS ACCIDENTAL

## INTERPOLATION AND EXTRAPOLATION
1 | Predicting within the range of the data is called this | INTERPOLATION / INTERPOLATING
2 | Predicting outside the range of the data is called this | EXTRAPOLATION | INTERPOLATION; REGRESSION; CORRELATION
3 | Which kind of prediction is usually more reliable? | INTERPOLATION | EXTRAPOLATION; THEY ARE EQUALLY RELIABLE; NEITHER IS EVER RELIABLE
4 | The line for a child's height is h = 6a + 80 (cm, age in years), based on ages 2 to 10. What height does it predict at age 8, in cm? | = 128 cm ~0
5 | Using h = 6a + 80, what height does it predict at age 40, and why is that unreliable? | 320 CM, BECAUSE PEOPLE STOP GROWING | 320 CM, BECAUSE THE SLOPE IS TOO SMALL; 240 CM, BECAUSE THE DATA ARE OLD; 320 CM, BECAUSE IT IS INTERPOLATION

## OUTLIERS IN TWO VARIABLES
1 | A point far from the trend of a scatter plot is called this | OUTLIER / AN OUTLIER
2 | Removing an outlier that lies far from the line usually does this to the strength of r (its size, ignoring the sign) | INCREASES IT | DECREASES IT; MAKES IT ZERO; MAKES IT NEGATIVE
3 | An outlier should only be removed if there is a good reason, such as this | A RECORDING OR MEASUREMENT ERROR | IT MAKES r SMALLER; IT DOESN'T FIT THE THEORY; IT IS THE LARGEST VALUE
4 | A point that greatly changes the slope of the regression line when removed is called this | AN INFLUENTIAL POINT | A RESIDUAL; THE MEAN; A LURKING VARIABLE
5 | Outliers in two-variable data are easiest to spot on this graph | A SCATTER PLOT | A PIE CHART; A BAR GRAPH; A STEM-AND-LEAF PLOT
