## THE BASICS
1 | A fair die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 5? (A fraction or a decimal) | = 1/6 ~1%
2 | Every possible outcome of an experiment, listed together, is called this | SAMPLE SPACE / THE SAMPLE SPACE
3 | Probabilities always fall between these two values | 0 AND 1 | −1 AND 1; 0 AND 100; 1 AND 10
4 | A card is drawn from a standard deck. What is the probability it is a face card (J, Q or K)? | = 12/52 ~1%
5 | Two coins are tossed. What is the probability of getting exactly one head? | = 1/2 ~1%

## ODDS
1 | The odds in favour of an event compare these two things | SUCCESSES TO FAILURES | SUCCESSES TO ALL OUTCOMES; FAILURES TO ALL OUTCOMES; TRIALS TO SUCCESSES
2 | The odds in favour of rolling a 6 on a fair die are this | 1 : 5 | 1 : 6; 5 : 1; 6 : 1
3 | The odds in favour of an event are 3 : 7. What is the probability of the event? | = 3/10 ~1%
4 | The probability of rain is 0.2. What are the odds against rain? | 4 : 1 | 1 : 4; 1 : 5; 5 : 1
5 | The odds against a horse winning are 9 : 2. What is the probability that it wins? | = 2/11 ~1%

## NOT HAPPENING
1 | The complement of event A, written A′, is this | A DOES NOT HAPPEN | A HAPPENS TWICE; A AND B BOTH HAPPEN; A IS CERTAIN
2 | P(A) = 0.35. What is P(A′)? | = 0.65 ~0.5%
3 | "At least one" problems are often easiest using this idea | THE COMPLEMENT | THE ADDITIVE PRINCIPLE; THE MEDIAN; ODDS AGAINST
4 | A coin is tossed 4 times. What is the probability of at least one head? | = 15/16 ~0.5%
5 | A die is rolled 4 times. To 3 decimal places, what is the probability of at least one 6? | = 0.518 ~0.5%

## CAN'T BOTH HAPPEN
1 | Events that cannot happen at the same time are called this | MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE / DISJOINT
2 | Which pair of events is mutually exclusive on one roll of a die? | ROLLING A 2 AND ROLLING A 5 | ROLLING AN EVEN AND ROLLING A 2; ROLLING UNDER 4 AND ROLLING AN ODD; ROLLING A 6 AND ROLLING OVER 3
3 | For mutually exclusive events, P(A or B) equals this | P(A) + P(B) | P(A) × P(B); P(A) + P(B) + P(A AND B); P(A) − P(B)
4 | For events that are not mutually exclusive, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) minus this | P(A AND B) | P(A) × P(B) TWICE; P(A′); 1
5 | A card is drawn. What is the probability it is a heart or a king? | = 16/52 ~1%

## NO INFLUENCE
1 | Events where one happening does not change the probability of the other | INDEPENDENT / INDEPENDENT EVENTS
2 | For independent events, P(A and B) equals this | P(A) × P(B) | P(A) + P(B); P(A) ÷ P(B); P(A) − P(B)
3 | A coin is tossed and a die is rolled. What is the probability of a head and a 6? | = 1/12 ~1%
4 | P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A and B) = 0.2. Are A and B independent? | YES | NO; ONLY IF THEY ARE MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE; IT CAN'T BE TOLD
5 | A free throw goes in 70% of the time, independently. What is the probability of making 3 in a row, to 3 decimal places? | = 0.343 ~0.5%

## ONE AFFECTS THE OTHER
1 | Drawing two cards without putting the first back makes the second draw this | DEPENDENT | INDEPENDENT; MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE; IMPOSSIBLE
2 | The probability of B given that A has happened is called this | CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY / CONDITIONAL
3 | The conditional probability P(B given A) equals this | P(A AND B) ÷ P(A) | P(A) × P(B); P(A) ÷ P(B); P(A OR B) ÷ P(B)
4 | Two cards are drawn without replacement. What is the probability both are aces? | = 1/221 ~1%
5 | P(A) = 0.5 and P(A and B) = 0.2. What is P(B given A)? | = 0.4 ~0.5%

## THEORY OR EXPERIMENT
1 | Probability found by actually doing trials, like flipping a coin 100 times, is called this | EXPERIMENTAL PROBABILITY | THEORETICAL PROBABILITY; SUBJECTIVE PROBABILITY; ODDS
2 | A coin lands heads 46 times in 100 tosses. What is the experimental probability of heads? | = 0.46 ~0.5%
3 | Probability based on an educated guess or opinion, like "I'm 80% sure it'll snow", is called this | SUBJECTIVE / SUBJECTIVE PROBABILITY
4 | As the number of trials grows, experimental probability gets closer to theoretical probability. This is the law of ___ | LARGE NUMBERS / LARGE NUMBER
5 | Running a model of an experiment many times on a computer or calculator is called this | A SIMULATION | A CENSUS; A PERMUTATION; A SURVEY

## TITANIC QUESTIONS
1 | Of 2200 passengers, 700 survived. To 2 decimal places, what is the experimental probability a passenger survived? | = 0.32 ~2%
2 | 300 of 450 women survived. What was the probability that a woman survived, to 2 decimal places? | = 0.67 ~1.5%
3 | "Women and children first" would be supported if P(survived given woman) were this compared with P(survived given man) | MUCH HIGHER | MUCH LOWER; EXACTLY EQUAL; ZERO
4 | If surviving were independent of being a woman, P(survived given woman) would equal this | P(SURVIVED) | P(WOMAN); 1; P(SURVIVED) × P(WOMAN)
5 | A table showing counts for two categorical variables, like sex and survival, is called this | A TWO-WAY TABLE / CONTINGENCY TABLE / TWO WAY TABLE
