## THE BASIC PROBLEM

## WHAT YOU GIVE UP

2 | Maya can work 3 hours at $15 an hour or go to a concert with a $40 ticket. What is the full opportunity cost of the concert, in dollars? | = 85 dollars ~0
3 | A cost that has already been paid and can't be recovered, so it should be ignored when deciding | SUNK COST / SUNK COSTS / SUNK
4 | Opportunity costs that involve no direct money payment, like an owner's forgone salary, are called these | IMPLICIT COSTS | EXPLICIT COSTS; SUNK COSTS; FIXED COSTS
5 | Moving along a bowed-out PPC, each extra unit of one good costs more and more of the other. This is the law of increasing ___ | OPPORTUNITY COST / OPPORTUNITY COSTS / COSTS / COST

## SHIFTING THE CURVE
1 | Better technology for making both goods does this to the PPC | SHIFTS IT OUTWARD | SHIFTS IT INWARD; MOVES THE ECONOMY ALONG IT; HAS NO EFFECT
2 | A natural disaster that destroys factories does this to the PPC | SHIFTS IT INWARD | SHIFTS IT OUTWARD; MOVES THE ECONOMY ALONG IT; MAKES IT A STRAIGHT LINE
3 | A straight-line PPC means this about opportunity cost | IT IS CONSTANT | IT IS INCREASING; IT IS DECREASING; IT IS ZERO
4 | A rise in unemployment moves the economy to here, without shifting the PPC | INSIDE THE CURVE | OUTSIDE THE CURVE; ALONG THE CURVE; TO THE ORIGIN
5 | A new technology that helps produce only wheat does this to a wheat-and-cloth PPC | PIVOTS IT OUT ALONG THE WHEAT AXIS ONLY | SHIFTS THE WHOLE CURVE OUTWARD; SHIFTS IT INWARD; LEAVES IT UNCHANGED

## WHO SHOULD MAKE WHAT?
1 | Being able to produce more of a good than another producer with the same resources | ABSOLUTE ADVANTAGE | COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE; ECONOMIES OF SCALE; SPECIALIZATION
2 | Being able to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost than another producer | COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE / COMPARATIVE
3 | Ana can make 20 shirts or 10 hats in a day. What is her opportunity cost of one hat, in shirts? | = 2 shirts ~0
4 | Ana can make 20 shirts or 10 hats in a day; Ben can make 12 shirts or 4 hats. Who has the comparative advantage in hats? | ANA | BEN; NEITHER; BOTH OF THEM
5 | Ana makes 20 shirts or 10 hats a day; Ben makes 12 shirts or 4 hats. What is Ben's opportunity cost of one shirt, in hats? Give a decimal. | = 0.333 hats ~2%

## COST-BENEFIT ANALYSIS
1 | A rational decision-maker takes an action whenever its benefit is at least as great as this | ITS COST | ITS PRICE; ITS SUNK COST; ZERO
2 | Costs that are actual money payments, like rent and wages, are called these | EXPLICIT COSTS / EXPLICIT COST / EXPLICIT
3 | Raj paid for a non-refundable $20 ticket, and the movie is boring. A rational Raj ignores the $20 because it is this | A SUNK COST | AN IMPLICIT COST; A MARGINAL COST; A VARIABLE COST
4 | A rational consumer spends their budget to maximize this | TOTAL UTILITY | MARGINAL UTILITY; TOTAL SPENDING; SAVINGS
5 | Studying 3 hours earns 30 points and studying 4 hours earns 36. What is the marginal benefit of the 4th hour, in points? | = 6 points ~0

## AT THE MARGIN
1 | The extra benefit from one more unit of something | MARGINAL BENEFIT | TOTAL BENEFIT; AVERAGE BENEFIT; NET BENEFIT
2 | The best amount of an activity is where marginal benefit equals this | MARGINAL COST / MC
3 | Each extra slice of pizza gives less satisfaction than the last. This is the law of diminishing ___ | MARGINAL UTILITY / UTILITY
4 | Hours 1 to 4 of tutoring have marginal benefits of $50, $40, $30 and $20, and each hour costs $35. How many hours should you buy? | = 2 hours ~0
5 | When marginal utility is negative, total utility is doing this | FALLING | RISING; AT ITS MAXIMUM; STAYING CONSTANT

## CONSUMER CHOICE
1 | Consumers maximize utility when the marginal utility per ___ spent is equal across goods | DOLLAR | UNIT; HOUR; GOOD
2 | The next slice of pizza costs $4 and gives 20 utils. What is its marginal utility per dollar? | = 5 utils ~0
3 | The next burger gives 30 utils for $6; the next taco gives 12 utils for $2. Which should you buy next? | THE TACO | THE BURGER; NEITHER; BOTH ARE EQUALLY GOOD
4 | Good X gives 8 utils per dollar at the margin and good Y gives 5. To raise total utility, the consumer should do this | BUY MORE X AND LESS Y | BUY MORE Y AND LESS X; KEEP THE SAME BUNDLE; BUY LESS OF BOTH
5 | Apples cost $1 and the last apple adds 6 utils. Oranges cost $2. For utility to be maximized, how many utils must the last orange add? | = 12 utils ~0
