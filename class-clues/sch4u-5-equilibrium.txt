## WHAT IS EQUILIBRIUM?
1 | At equilibrium, the forward and reverse rates are this | EQUAL | ZERO; AS FAST AS POSSIBLE; DIFFERENT
2 | Equilibrium is called ___ because reactions keep happening in both directions | DYNAMIC
3 | At equilibrium, the concentrations of reactants and products are this | CONSTANT | EQUAL; ZERO; ALWAYS CHANGING
4 | Equilibrium can only be reached in this kind of system | CLOSED | OPEN; ISOLATED FROM LIGHT; COLD
5 | Which reaction goes to completion rather than reaching equilibrium? | BURNING METHANE IN AIR | N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2 IN A SEALED FLASK; H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI; DISSOLVING SALT IN A SATURATED SOLUTION

## EQUILIBRIUM EXPRESSIONS
1 | For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, K = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ ÷ this | [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ | [A][B]; [C][D]; [A]ᵇ[B]ᵃ
2 | Which substances are left out of an equilibrium expression? | PURE SOLIDS AND LIQUIDS | GASES; IONS IN SOLUTION; PRODUCTS
3 | For N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, what is the exponent on [H2] in K? | = 3 ~0
4 | [N2] = 0.50, [H2] = 0.30 and [NH3] = 0.20 mol/L at equilibrium. To 2 decimal places, what is K? | = 2.96 ~1%
5 | K = 4 for A ⇌ B. What is K for B ⇌ A? | = 0.25 ~0

## SIZE OF K
1 | A very large K (like 10¹⁰) means the equilibrium lies this way | TOWARD PRODUCTS | TOWARD REACTANTS; IN THE MIDDLE; IT DOESN'T MEAN ANYTHING
2 | A very small K means mostly these are present at equilibrium | REACTANTS | PRODUCTS; CATALYSTS; IONS
3 | K = 3 for A ⇌ 2B. What is K for 2A ⇌ 4B? | = 9 ~0
4 | Does adding a catalyst change K? | NO | YES, IT INCREASES K; YES, IT DECREASES K; ONLY FOR GASES
5 | The only thing that changes the value of K for a reaction is this | TEMPERATURE | CONCENTRATION; PRESSURE; A CATALYST

## THE REACTION QUOTIENT
1 | Q has the same form as K but uses concentrations at this moment | ANY MOMENT (NOT NECESSARILY EQUILIBRIUM) | EQUILIBRIUM ONLY; THE START ONLY; ZERO
2 | If Q < K, the reaction shifts this way | TOWARD PRODUCTS (FORWARD) | TOWARD REACTANTS; IT DOESN'T SHIFT; IT STOPS
3 | If Q > K, the reaction shifts this way | TOWARD REACTANTS (REVERSE) | TOWARD PRODUCTS; IT DOESN'T SHIFT; IT SPEEDS UP
4 | For A ⇌ 2B with K = 1, [A] = 0.20 and [B] = 0.40 mol/L. What is Q? | = 0.8 ~0.5%
5 | For A ⇌ 2B with K = 1 and Q = 0.8, which way does the reaction shift? | TOWARD B | TOWARD A; NO SHIFT; IT CAN'T BE TOLD

## LE CHÂTELIER'S PRINCIPLE
1 | When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to do this | REDUCE THE DISTURBANCE | MAKE IT WORSE; STOP REACTING; DOUBLE THE PRODUCTS
2 | Adding more reactant shifts the equilibrium this way | TOWARD PRODUCTS | TOWARD REACTANTS; NO SHIFT; IT STOPS
3 | For N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, increasing the pressure shifts it this way | TOWARD NH3 | TOWARD N2 AND H2; NO SHIFT; IT DEPENDS ON THE CATALYST
4 | For an exothermic reaction, raising the temperature shifts it this way | TOWARD REACTANTS | TOWARD PRODUCTS; NO SHIFT; IT STOPS
5 | For H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI (all gases), increasing the pressure does this | NOTHING (EQUAL MOLES OF GAS ON EACH SIDE) | SHIFTS TOWARD HI; SHIFTS TOWARD H2 AND I2; DOUBLES K

## ICE TABLES
1 | ICE stands for Initial, Change and this | EQUILIBRIUM
2 | For H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI, if H2 changes by −x, how does HI change? | +2x | +x; −2x; −x
3 | H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI, K = 64. Starting with 0.50 mol/L each of H2 and I2, what is [HI] at equilibrium, in mol/L? | = 0.8 ~0.5%
4 | For that same reaction, what is [H2] at equilibrium, in mol/L? | = 0.1 ~1%
5 | PCl5 ⇌ PCl3 + Cl2, K = 0.040. Starting with 1.0 mol/L PCl5, to 2 decimal places what is [Cl2] at equilibrium? | = 0.18 ~2%

## SOLUBILITY EQUILIBRIUM
1 | The equilibrium constant for a slightly soluble salt dissolving | Ksp / SOLUBILITY PRODUCT / SOLUBILITY PRODUCT CONSTANT
2 | For AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻, Ksp equals this | [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] | [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] ÷ [AgCl]; [Ag⁺] + [Cl⁻]; [AgCl]
3 | Ksp for AgCl is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. What is its molar solubility, in mol/L? | = 1.3e-5 ~3%
4 | PbI2 has a solubility of 1.5 × 10⁻³ mol/L. What is its Ksp? (Ksp = 4s³) | = 1.35e-8 ~1%
5 | Ksp for BaSO4 is 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰. What is its molar solubility, in mol/L? | = 1.05e-5 ~2%

## PRECIPITATES
1 | A precipitate forms when the ion product Q is this compared with Ksp | GREATER | LESS; EQUAL ONLY; ZERO
2 | [Ag⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ and [Cl⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L (Ksp of AgCl = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰). Will a precipitate form? | YES | NO; ONLY IF HEATED; IT CAN'T BE TOLD
3 | Adding NaCl to a saturated AgCl solution makes less AgCl dissolve. This is the ___ ion effect | COMMON
4 | What is the solubility of AgCl in 0.10 mol/L NaCl, in mol/L? (Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰) | = 1.8e-9 ~1%
5 | A solution that holds exactly as much solute as it can at that temperature | SATURATED

## INDUSTRY AND LIFE
1 | The Haber process makes this compound | AMMONIA / NH3
2 | The Haber process uses high pressure because it shifts the equilibrium toward this | AMMONIA (FEWER GAS MOLES) | NITROGEN; HYDROGEN; HEAT
3 | Haber plants run at about 450°C even though lower temperatures give more NH3. Why? | THE REACTION IS FASTER | K IS LARGER; IT IS SAFER; NH3 IS A LIQUID THERE
4 | CO binds to hemoglobin so strongly that it shifts this equilibrium | OXYGEN TRANSPORT IN BLOOD | DIGESTION; PHOTOSYNTHESIS; THE WATER CYCLE
5 | Stalactites form as dissolved calcium carbonate does this when CO2 escapes | PRECIPITATES OUT | DISSOLVES MORE; EVAPORATES; BURNS

## K WITH GASES
1 | Kp uses these instead of concentrations | PARTIAL PRESSURES | MASSES; VOLUMES; TEMPERATURES
2 | Adding an inert gas like argon at constant volume does this to the equilibrium | NOTHING | SHIFTS IT TO PRODUCTS; SHIFTS IT TO REACTANTS; DOUBLES K
3 | Decreasing the volume of a gas mixture shifts it toward the side with this | FEWER MOLES OF GAS | MORE MOLES OF GAS; MORE PRODUCTS; HIGHER ENERGY
4 | For CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g), K equals this | [CO2] | [CaO][CO2] ÷ [CaCO3]; [CaCO3]; 1
5 | For 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3, how many moles of gas are on the reactant side? | = 3 ~0

## TEMPERATURE AND K
1 | For an endothermic reaction, raising the temperature does this to K | INCREASES IT | DECREASES IT; NOTHING; MAKES IT NEGATIVE
2 | For an exothermic reaction, lowering the temperature does this to K | INCREASES IT | DECREASES IT; NOTHING; MAKES IT ZERO
3 | N2O4 (colourless) ⇌ 2NO2 (brown) is endothermic. Heating the flask makes it look this | DARKER BROWN | LIGHTER; COLOURLESS; BLUE
4 | Cooling that flask shifts the equilibrium toward this | N2O4 | NO2; NEITHER; BOTH EQUALLY
5 | In Le Châtelier problems, you can treat heat as a reactant or product. In an exothermic reaction it is a ___ | PRODUCT
