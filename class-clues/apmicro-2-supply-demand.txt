## WHAT BUYERS WANT
1 | The law of demand: when the price of a good rises, this falls | QUANTITY DEMANDED | DEMAND; SUPPLY; QUANTITY SUPPLIED
2 | A change in the good's own price causes this | A MOVEMENT ALONG THE DEMAND CURVE | A SHIFT OF THE DEMAND CURVE; A SHIFT OF THE SUPPLY CURVE; NO CHANGE AT ALL
3 | When incomes rise, demand for this kind of good falls, like bus rides or instant noodles | INFERIOR GOOD / INFERIOR GOODS / INFERIOR
4 | When the price of coffee rises, demand for cream falls. Coffee and cream are these | COMPLEMENTS | SUBSTITUTES; INFERIOR GOODS; NORMAL GOODS
5 | Buyers expect laptop prices to rise next month. Today's demand for laptops does this | INCREASES | DECREASES; STAYS THE SAME; BECOMES PERFECTLY INELASTIC

## WHAT SELLERS OFFER
1 | The law of supply: a higher price leads to more of this | QUANTITY SUPPLIED | SUPPLY; DEMAND; QUANTITY DEMANDED
2 | A rise in the price of an input, like steel for car makers, does this to supply | DECREASES IT | INCREASES IT; HAS NO EFFECT; INCREASES DEMAND
3 | Better technology that lowers production costs shifts the supply curve this way | RIGHT / TO THE RIGHT / RIGHTWARD / OUTWARD
4 | A per-unit subsidy to producers does this to supply | INCREASES IT | DECREASES IT; HAS NO EFFECT; MAKES IT PERFECTLY ELASTIC
5 | A farmer can grow corn or soybeans. If the price of soybeans rises, the supply of corn does this | DECREASES | INCREASES; STAYS THE SAME; BECOMES MORE ELASTIC
