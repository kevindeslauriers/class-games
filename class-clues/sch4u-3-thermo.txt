## ENERGY WORDS
1 | A reaction that releases heat to its surroundings | EXOTHERMIC | ENDOTHERMIC; ISOTHERMAL; ADIABATIC
2 | A reaction that absorbs heat from its surroundings | ENDOTHERMIC
3 | The heat content of a system at constant pressure | ENTHALPY | ENTROPY; KINETIC ENERGY; ACTIVATION ENERGY
4 | For an exothermic reaction, ΔH is this | NEGATIVE | POSITIVE; ZERO; UNDEFINED
5 | Melting ice is this kind of process | ENDOTHERMIC | EXOTHERMIC; NEITHER; BOTH

## q = mcΔT
1 | In q = mcΔT, what does c stand for? | SPECIFIC HEAT CAPACITY | CONCENTRATION; THE SPEED OF LIGHT; CHARGE
2 | How much heat, in J, raises 100 g of water from 25°C to 35°C? | = 4180 J ~0.5%
3 | 50 g of a metal absorbs 450 J and warms by 20°C. What is its specific heat, in J/(g·°C)? | = 0.45 ~1%
4 | 200 g of water absorbs 8360 J. By how many degrees does it warm? | = 10 °C ~0.5%
5 | Which heats up faster with the same heat added: 1 kg of water or 1 kg of iron? | IRON | WATER; THEY HEAT EQUALLY; IT DEPENDS ON THE SHAPE

## CALORIMETRY
1 | A device that measures the heat of a reaction | CALORIMETER / A CALORIMETER
2 | In a coffee-cup calorimeter, heat released by the reaction is absorbed mainly by this | THE WATER | THE CUP; THE AIR; THE THERMOMETER
3 | 2.00 g of NaOH (molar mass 40.0 g/mol) dissolves in 100 g of water, which warms by 5.0°C. What is ΔH of solution, in kJ/mol? | = -41.8 kJ/mol ~1%
4 | A calorimeter that keeps the volume constant, used for combustion, is called this | A BOMB CALORIMETER | A COFFEE-CUP CALORIMETER; A BUNSEN BURNER; A BURETTE
5 | If heat is lost to the surroundings, the measured ΔH will be this compared with the true value | SMALLER IN SIZE | LARGER IN SIZE; THE SAME; POSITIVE INSTEAD OF NEGATIVE

## HESS'S LAW
1 | Hess's law says ΔH for a reaction is the same no matter this | WHICH STEPS (PATH) ARE TAKEN | THE TEMPERATURE; THE CATALYST USED; HOW FAST IT GOES
2 | If you reverse a reaction, ΔH does this | CHANGES SIGN | STAYS THE SAME; DOUBLES; BECOMES ZERO
3 | If you multiply a reaction by 2, ΔH does this | DOUBLES | STAYS THE SAME; CHANGES SIGN; HALVES
4 | C + O2 → CO2, ΔH = −393.5 kJ. CO + ½O2 → CO2, ΔH = −283.0 kJ. What is ΔH for C + ½O2 → CO, in kJ? | = -110.5 kJ ~0.5%
5 | A → B has ΔH = +50 kJ and B → C has ΔH = −80 kJ. What is ΔH for A → C, in kJ? | = -30 kJ ~0

## HEATS OF FORMATION
1 | The standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state is this | ZERO | ONE; NEGATIVE; POSITIVE
2 | ΔH = ΣnΔHf(products) − this | ΣnΔHf(REACTANTS) | ΣnΔHf(PRODUCTS); ΔHf OF WATER; ACTIVATION ENERGY
3 | ΔHf: CH4 = −74.8, CO2 = −393.5, H2O(l) = −285.8 kJ/mol. What is ΔH for CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O(l), in kJ? | = -890.3 kJ ~0.5%
4 | The standard conditions for ΔHf values are 25°C and this pressure | 100 kPa | 0 kPa; 1000 kPa; 50 kPa
5 | What is ΔHf for O2(g)? | = 0 ~0

## BOND ENERGIES
1 | Breaking bonds ___ energy | ABSORBS / REQUIRES / TAKES / USES
2 | Making bonds ___ energy | RELEASES | ABSORBS; DESTROYS; STORES
3 | ΔH = bonds broken − bonds formed. H–H = 436, Cl–Cl = 243, H–Cl = 432 kJ/mol. What is ΔH for H2 + Cl2 → 2HCl, in kJ? | = -185 kJ ~0
4 | If the bonds formed are stronger than those broken, the reaction is this | EXOTHERMIC | ENDOTHERMIC; NEITHER; IMPOSSIBLE
5 | Bond energy calculations are less accurate than ΔHf ones because bond energies are this | AVERAGES | EXACT; MEASURED IN WATER; ALWAYS ZERO

## THERMOCHEMICAL EQUATIONS
1 | 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O + 572 kJ. Is this exothermic or endothermic? | EXOTHERMIC | ENDOTHERMIC; NEITHER; IT CAN'T BE TOLD
2 | 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, ΔH = −572 kJ. How much heat is released per mole of H2O formed, in kJ? | = 286 kJ ~0.5%
3 | CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O, ΔH = −890 kJ. How much heat comes from burning 2.0 mol of CH4, in kJ? | = 1780 kJ ~0.5%
4 | Burning 16.0 g of CH4 (molar mass 16.0 g/mol) releases how much heat, in kJ? (ΔH = −890 kJ/mol) | = 890 kJ ~0.5%
5 | Photosynthesis is endothermic. Where does its energy come from? | SUNLIGHT | THE SOIL; OXYGEN; GLUCOSE
