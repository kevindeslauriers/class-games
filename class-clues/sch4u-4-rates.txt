## MEASURING RATE
1 | A reaction rate is the change in concentration divided by this | TIME / THE CHANGE IN TIME
2 | [A] drops from 0.50 to 0.30 mol/L in 40 s. What is the average rate, in mol/(L·s)? | = 0.005 ~0.5%
3 | A rate found from the slope of a tangent line at one moment is the ___ rate | INSTANTANEOUS
4 | For 2A → B, if A is used up at 0.10 mol/(L·s), how fast is B made? | = 0.05 ~0.5%
5 | Which is a way to measure the rate of a reaction that makes a gas? | MEASURE THE GAS VOLUME OVER TIME | WEIGH THE BEAKER ONCE; MEASURE THE FINAL pH ONLY; COUNT THE BUBBLES AT THE END

## WHAT AFFECTS RATE
1 | Raising the temperature does this to the reaction rate | INCREASES IT | DECREASES IT; HAS NO EFFECT; STOPS IT
2 | Grinding a solid into powder speeds a reaction by increasing this | SURFACE AREA | ACTIVATION ENERGY; ΔH; MASS
3 | A substance that speeds up a reaction without being used up | CATALYST / A CATALYST
4 | Increasing the concentration of reactants increases the rate by causing more of these | COLLISIONS | PRODUCTS AT ONCE; CATALYSTS; BONDS
5 | Biological catalysts made of protein | ENZYMES / ENZYME

## COLLISION THEORY
1 | For a reaction to happen, particles must collide with enough energy and this | THE RIGHT ORIENTATION | THE SAME MASS; OPPOSITE CHARGES; THE SAME SPEED
2 | The minimum energy needed for a successful collision | ACTIVATION ENERGY / Ea
3 | The high-energy arrangement at the top of the energy barrier | THE ACTIVATED COMPLEX (TRANSITION STATE) | THE PRODUCT; THE CATALYST; THE INTERMEDIATE
4 | A catalyst speeds a reaction by doing this | PROVIDING A PATH WITH LOWER ACTIVATION ENERGY | RAISING THE TEMPERATURE; CHANGING ΔH; ADDING MORE REACTANTS
5 | Raising temperature speeds reactions mainly because more particles have this | ENERGY ABOVE THE ACTIVATION ENERGY | MORE MASS; MORE BONDS; LOWER ΔH

## HALF-LIFE
1 | The time for half of a reactant to be used up | HALF-LIFE / HALF LIFE
2 | For a first-order reaction, the half-life does this as concentration falls | STAYS THE SAME | GETS LONGER; GETS SHORTER; DOUBLES EACH TIME
3 | A first-order reaction has k = 0.0231 per second. To the nearest second, what is its half-life? | = 30 s ~1%
4 | After 3 half-lives, what fraction of a reactant remains? (A fraction or a decimal) | = 1/8 ~0.5%
5 | A first-order reaction's half-life is 10 min. Starting at 0.80 mol/L, what is the concentration after 30 min, in mol/L? | = 0.1 ~0.5%
